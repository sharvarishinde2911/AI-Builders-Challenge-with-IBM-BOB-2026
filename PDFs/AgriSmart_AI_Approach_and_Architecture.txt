AGRISMART
AI APPROACH AND SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE

1. PROJECT OVERVIEW

AgriSmart is an AI-powered, satellite-based crop monitoring platform designed to help farmers detect crop problems early.

The system combines satellite imagery, weather information, crop images, AI analysis, and market information to provide simple and useful recommendations to farmers.

Main objective:
Monitor crops -> Detect problems early -> Alert the farmer -> Suggest appropriate action.

2. AI APPROACH

AgriSmart can use a combination of AI, computer vision, satellite-data analysis, and rule-based decision logic.

A. SATELLITE CROP MONITORING

Satellite imagery can be used to monitor the condition of agricultural fields.

The system can use Sentinel-2 or similar satellite data when connected to a real satellite-data provider.

Important vegetation indicators include:

- NDVI (Normalized Difference Vegetation Index)
- NDWI (Normalized Difference Water Index)
- Other vegetation and moisture indicators when required

NDVI can be calculated using:

NDVI = (NIR - Red) / (NIR + Red)

Higher NDVI generally indicates healthier and more actively growing vegetation, while lower values can indicate crop stress, bare soil, or damaged vegetation.

AI Approach:
1. Collect satellite images for the selected field.
2. Preprocess the satellite data.
3. Calculate vegetation and moisture indicators.
4. Compare current values with previous observations.
5. Detect unusual changes.
6. Classify the field as Healthy, Attention, or Critical.
7. Send an alert when significant stress is detected.

B. CROP HEALTH DETECTION

The crop health module converts satellite indicators and other available information into an easy-to-understand status.

Example:

Healthy:
Normal vegetation condition and stable indicators.

Attention:
Possible early stress or abnormal change.

Critical:
Strong or continuous stress requiring immediate attention.

For a prototype, threshold-based classification can be used. Later, a machine-learning model can be trained using historical crop and satellite data.

C. WATER STRESS AND DROUGHT DETECTION

Water stress can be estimated by combining:

- NDVI
- NDWI
- Rainfall
- Temperature
- Humidity
- Historical field observations

AI can compare current conditions with normal conditions for the crop and location.

If vegetation health decreases while moisture and rainfall indicators are also low, the system can generate a possible water-stress alert.

D. WEATHER-BASED AI ALERTS

The platform can obtain weather information such as:

- Temperature
- Rainfall
- Humidity
- Wind
- Weather warnings

A decision engine can combine weather data with crop information.

Example:
If heavy rainfall is predicted and the crop is sensitive to excess moisture, the system can display a crop-aware warning.

E. CROP IMAGE DIAGNOSIS

Farmers can upload a photo of a crop or leaf.

The image-analysis pipeline can be:

A CNN or modern vision model can be used for crop disease classification.

For the initial prototype, a pretrained image-classification model can be used. Later, it can be fine-tuned using region-specific crop disease datasets.

The result should be presented as a possible diagnosis rather than a guaranteed diagnosis.

F. MARKET INFORMATION

Market data can be collected from an appropriate agricultural market-data source.

AI can help summarize:

- Current price
- Recent price trend
- Price comparison between markets
- Simple selling-related insights

The system should clearly distinguish factual market data from AI-generated interpretation.

Recommended Action

For a hackathon prototype, a hybrid approach is recommended:

- Use real APIs where available.
- Use simulated satellite data if live satellite integration is not available.
- Use simple thresholds for NDVI/NDWI-based demonstrations.
- Use a pretrained or demo computer-vision model for crop images.
- Use a rule-based recommendation engine for transparent alerts.
- Add an AI/LLM layer for explaining results in simple language.

9. AI EXPLANATION LAYER

An AI language model can convert technical analysis into simple farmer-friendly language.

Example:

Technical result:
NDVI decreased significantly over the last observation period.

Farmer-friendly result:
"Your crop may be experiencing stress. Check soil moisture and irrigation conditions."

The explanation can be generated in:

- English
- Marathi
- Hindi

The AI explanation should use the actual detected data as input instead of inventing measurements.

10. END-TO-END WORKFLOW

Step 1:
Farmer selects or registers a field.

Step 2:
The system stores the field location and crop information.

Step 3:
Satellite and weather data are collected for the field.

Step 4:
The system calculates crop-health and moisture indicators.

Step 5:
AI/rule-based analysis identifies possible crop stress.

Step 6:
The decision engine assigns a status:
Healthy, Attention, or Critical.

Step 7:
Weather conditions are checked for crop-specific risks.

Step 8:
If the farmer uploads a crop image, the computer-vision module analyzes it.

Step 9:
The system combines the results and generates an easy-to-understand alert.

Step 10:
The farmer receives a recommended next action.

14. FINAL AI APPROACH

AgriSmart should use a HYBRID AI APPROACH.

Satellite analysis provides field-level crop monitoring.
Computer vision provides crop-image diagnosis.
Weather and market APIs provide external information.
Machine-learning models identify patterns and risks.
A decision engine combines these results.
An AI language layer explains the results in simple English, Marathi, or Hindi.

This approach is practical for a hackathon because it allows the team to demonstrate the complete workflow even when live satellite or agricultural datasets are limited.
